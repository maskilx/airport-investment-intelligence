## In California multi-airport CBSAs, most measured secondary airports have a lower departure delay rate than the volume leader.

Passenger figures are observed T-100 enplanements. The LAX departure delay rate is calculated. Gaps versus each CBSA volume leader are calculated. The window is 2025-09…2026-07, the overlap of T-100 and On-Time coverage. The primary airport in each CBSA is the volume leader. The rank is the calculated gap (secondary delay rate minus the leader's delay rate), from the largest gap to the smallest. It is not an investment screen.

Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim observed enplanements were 42.16M passengers. LAX, the volume leader, had 32.74M passengers. SNA had 5.02M passengers. BUR had 2.78M passengers. LGB had 1.62M passengers.

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont observed enplanements were 27.84M passengers. SFO, the volume leader, had 24.08M passengers. OAK had 3.75M passengers. CCR had 11,353 passengers.

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad observed enplanements were 12.36M passengers. SAN, the volume leader, had 12.26M passengers. CLD had 97,601 passengers.

Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario observed enplanements were 4.79M passengers. ONT, the volume leader, had 3.19M passengers. PSP had 1.57M passengers. SBD had 30,634 passengers.

Santa Maria-Santa Barbara observed enplanements were 710,252 passengers. SBA, the volume leader, had 686,117 passengers. SMX had 24,135 passengers.

Calculated departure delay rates for the volume leaders were LAX 20.5%, SFO 24.6%, SAN 25.8%, ONT 19.1%, and SBA 18.0%.

Secondaries with a calculated delay rate, from the largest gap versus the volume leader to the smallest: PSP, SMX, SNA, OAK, BUR, LGB, CLD. There are no ties.

PSP delay rate 19.5% minus ONT delay rate 19.1% equals gap 0.4%, a proportional difference of 2.3% relative to ONT.

SMX delay rate 18.2% minus SBA delay rate 18.0% equals gap 0.2%, a proportional difference of 1.2% relative to SBA.

SNA delay rate 20.2% minus LAX delay rate 20.5% equals gap -0.2%, a proportional difference of -1.1% relative to LAX.

OAK delay rate 24.4% minus SFO delay rate 24.6% equals gap -0.3%, a proportional difference of -1.1% relative to SFO.

BUR delay rate 19.4% minus LAX delay rate 20.5% equals gap -1.1%, a proportional difference of -5.5% relative to LAX.

LGB delay rate 17.7% minus LAX delay rate 20.5% equals gap -2.8%, a proportional difference of -13.5% relative to LAX.

CLD delay rate 19.1% minus SAN delay rate 25.8% equals gap -6.7%, a proportional difference of -25.9% relative to SAN. The CLD figures carry a low On-Time coverage caveat.

CCR has no calculated departure delay rate in this window, and On-Time coverage at CCR is n/a (not established). SBD has no calculated departure delay rate in this window, and On-Time coverage at SBD is n/a (not established). CCR and SBD are omitted from the rank.

SJC is in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara CBSA, not the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont CBSA, so SJC is not treated as a secondary to SFO. CBSAs with a single airport in the threshold set are outside this rank.

**Assumptions**
- The primary airport in a CBSA is the member with the highest observed enplaned passengers over 2025-09…2026-07. Hub class on the airport finding labels was not the selection rule.
- A multi-airport CBSA is a home CBSA with multiple California airports at or above the catalog commercial-enplanement threshold used for entity resolution. Airports below that threshold are excluded.
- Geography is the airport home CBSA. Combined statistical areas are not merged, so SJC stays in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara and is not a secondary to SFO.
- The rank uses the calculated difference of departure delay rates, secondary minus volume leader. A positive gap means a higher delay rate than the leader. The proportional difference expresses that gap relative to the leader's rate.
- The shared window is 2025-09…2026-07. Later On-Time months are outside it because T-100 passenger coverage does not include them.

**Methodology**
- `passengers`: Σ passengers on departing passenger/combi segments
- `dep_delay15_rate`: departures ≥15 min late / operated departures
- `derived:difference`: difference of [Departure delay rate (≥15 min) — SNA, cbsa=Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim, CA, hub_class=M — 2025-09…2026-07] , [Departure delay rate (≥15 min) — LAX, cbsa=Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim, CA, hub_class=L — 2025-09…2026-07]
- `derived:pct_difference`: pct_difference of [Departure delay rate (≥15 min) — PSP, cbsa=Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario, CA, hub_class=S — 2025-09…2026-07] , [Departure delay rate (≥15 min) — ONT, cbsa=Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario, CA, hub_class=M — 2025-09…2026-07]
- `ontime_coverage`: operated reporting-carrier departures (On-Time) / passenger departures (T-100), same window

**Caveats and limitations**
- T-100 passengers are segment enplanements (connecting passengers count at each enplanement).
- On-Time covers domestic flights of reporting carriers only (no international, no small carriers).
- On-Time covers less than 70% of this airport's airline departures; delay/peak figures describe a partial slice.
- Departure delay rates cover domestic flights of reporting carriers only. International flights and non-reporting carriers are outside the rate.
- CLD delay rate 19.1% and gap -6.7% carry a low On-Time coverage caveat, so that comparison is a partial slice.
- CCR and SBD cannot be ranked: On-Time coverage is not established, and no departure delay rate was produced.
- Enplanements are T-100 segment passengers. A connecting passenger counts at each enplanement. They are not an origin-and-destination catchment total.
- A CBSA is an administrative boundary, not a measured passenger catchment. Travelers cross CBSA borders, including between the San Francisco and San Jose CBSAs and between the Los Angeles and Inland Empire CBSAs.
- This ordering is a delay comparison. It is not an investment screen, so there are no screening flags or scenario weights. The delay-gap order has no ties.
- Unmet demand, terminal capacity, fares, and profitability are not measured. A lower delay rate does not establish spare capacity, unmet demand, or an investment return. No forecast or estimated series enters this comparison.
